# SOLIDWORKS AI Just Got Better - Here's What's New in FD03

**[0:01]** Hey, it's Noah. Back with some new and exciting SOLIDWORKS enhancements. But this isn't just any what's new video. We know AI has been a major topic, and designers and engineers want to know how AI can actually improve CAD workflows. That's why we've put together a steady stream of AI focus updates rolling out over the next several releases. So without further ado, welcome to our first update on AI in SOLIDWORKS Design. Things are progressing fast, and we're starting with an important shift in how you interact with these tools. When we initially introduced AI capabilities, it was through a list of individual skills you can invoke with specific prompts. In FDO3, we've moved to a streamlined unified interface. This isn't just about a new look. It's a more intuitive experience where you can have a natural conversation with AI that understands your intent and seamlessly activates the right skill to get you to your goal. You can also use multiple skills in a single conversation to

**[1:01]** move through a complex workflow without jumping between menus. Plus, if you're looking for a specific capability, our new prompt library acts as your roadmap for everything AI powered in SOLIDWORKS. Engineers are constantly searching for ways to reduce repetitive work, so we focused on streamlining one of the most powerful ways designers can automate workflows, with macro creation. You no longer need to be a coding expert to group together certain keystrokes or clicks into a single command. By simply describing a task in natural language, integrated AI generates a VBA macro and opens it directly in your editor. Whether you want to automate file exports or custom properties, you can run the code immediately or refine it through chat, making design automation accessible to everyone. Another way we've minimized tedious work is by leveraging AI to help with multiple selections. You can now invoke a selection assistant. Instead of manually clicking dozens of items, you can use

**[2:01]** natural language to isolate parts across sketches and drawings, reducing mistakes and saving time. If there is one area that consistently impacts designers, its large assembly workflows. Previously, we introduced the ability to run a performance analysis for your assembly. Now you can act on that feedback directly. For example, highlighting major culprits of high triangle counts like modeled threads. SOLIDWORKS AI can identify and simplify these parts across your entire assembly in a single step. To further optimize performance of your large assemblies, we've introduced AI guided speed pack generation. This will reduce assembly lag without the manual burden of opening and inspecting every single component. Just ask AI to create lightweight representations for your top level or subassemblies, giving you a practical, automated way to reduce complexity when modeling, and keep your large assemblies running fast.

**[3:01]** Finally, we've improved upon one of the most requested use cases for AI and CAD. The ability to transform B-Rep geometry like step files into fully editable parametric parts. We've all dealt with imported geometry that's hard to modify from its original state. Now you can tap into artificial intelligence to convert this geometry for greater design flexibility. This is ideal for reverse engineering or iterating on existing CAD, making your imported files just as functional as the ones you built from scratch. And that rounds up our AI preview for this latest release of SOLIDWORKS Design. As always, there's plenty more to explore included in this and previous releases. So for a deeper look at how AI is improving CAD workflows, visit SOLIDWORKS.com/AI. We'll see you at the next one.
