# Task chaining

Linking several adjacent tasks in a workflow so that AI executes them as one continuous sequence, instead of using AI for isolated steps. How much a chain is worth depends on every link: one step that is hard for AI can break it.

## What the sources say

- Work is better modelled as sequences of interdependent tasks than as a list of separate tasks. AI's value depends on how those tasks are sequenced and connected. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]
- When adjacent tasks are all well suited to AI, they can be bundled into a chain. A single step that is hard for AI can undermine the whole chain. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]
- Design principle: how tasks are clustered matters as much as which tasks are automated. AI-friendly tasks that are scattered, or interrupted by tasks AI struggles with, deliver less benefit. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]
- Occupations with similar tasks can differ in how chainable they are. Lecture-based teaching, where content is prepared in advance, allows more automation than tutoring, where the work is a continuous back-and-forth with the student. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]
- The idea comes from the paper *Chaining Tasks, Redefining Work: A Theory of AI Automation* (Shahidi, Demirer, Horton, Immorlica, Lucier). The wiki so far has only a news article about it, not the paper. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]
