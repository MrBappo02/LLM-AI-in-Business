# Human-AI handoff costs

The coordination cost incurred each time work passes between AI and a human, who must review, validate and adjust it. Because these checkpoints slow the whole system, cutting handoffs can be worth more than getting each step done as well as possible.

## What the sources say

- Each handoff from AI to a human requires review, validation and adjustment, and these checkpoints slow the overall system. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]
- AI does not need to outperform humans at every task to create value. Assigning a whole chain to AI can pay off even if humans would do some steps better, because the human time saved on oversight can outweigh small differences in quality. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]
- Leaders should therefore judge AI by whether it improves the efficiency of the entire workflow, not by whether it excels at each individual step. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]

## Debates

**Should SMEs cut human checkpoints, or keep a human approving every output?**

- **Fewer handoffs.** MIT Sloan reports that handing a whole chain of tasks to AI can beat per-step quality, because each AI-to-human review costs time. Leaders should judge AI on whole-workflow efficiency. [[2026-04-22-how-ai-is-reshaping-workflows-and-redefining-jobs]]
- **A human approves everything.** Three AIBS handbook chapters require a named person to approve every AI quote or design and treat every output as a draft. [[2026-10-05-aibs-handbook-themes-1-2]] [[2026-09-24-aibs-handbook-trust-ai-safety]] [[2026-10-12-aibs-handbook-geopolitics]] The Trust chapter allows AI to act alone only for low-risk, reversible tasks, and only after it has climbed an autonomy ladder. [[2026-09-24-aibs-handbook-trust-ai-safety]]
- **What it turns on:**
  - *Stakes and reversibility.* A wrong quote, a misread tolerance or a leaked NDA drawing is costly for a small firm. [[2026-09-24-aibs-handbook-trust-ai-safety]]
  - *Legal limits.* GDPR Art. 22 bars leaving decisions that seriously affect a person to AI alone. [[2026-09-24-aibs-handbook-trust-ai-safety]]
  - *Stage of adoption.* The handbooks address a first pilot, while the MIT argument concerns redesigned workflows at scale.
  - *Different kinds of source.* The MIT claim comes from a theory paper as reported in a news article. The handbooks are practical guidance that relays other sources. Neither tests the trade-off empirically.
- The two positions may be compatible: the handbooks' ladder could be the route by which a firm reaches MIT-style end-to-end chains. No source in the wiki says so yet.
