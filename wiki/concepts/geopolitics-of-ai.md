# Geopolitics of AI

The competition between the US, China and the EU to control the AI stack (chips, computing, models and software), and what that competition means for firms that buy AI tools. For an SME it shows up as vendor risk and as competitors on a different technology stack.

## What the sources say

- The US, China and the EU each want to own the AI stack, and each says so in official plans. [[2026-10-12-aibs-handbook-geopolitics]]
- The US frames AI as a race for global dominance and wants to export its full AI technology stack to allies. Allies are expected to follow US export controls, or face possible secondary tariffs (relayed from *America's AI Action Plan*, 2025). [[2026-10-12-aibs-handbook-geopolitics]]
- China's 15th Five-Year Plan targets integrated circuits, machine tools, industrial software and AI chips for an independent, controllable ecosystem, and its AI+ initiative applies AI across the industrial life cycle, including design. Chinese machine builders will compete with AI-supported design on a separate stack. [[2026-10-12-aibs-handbook-geopolitics]]
- The EU puts open-source software at the centre of its technological independence to reduce lock-in to non-EU vendors, and funds made-in-Europe generative AI through [[genai4eu]]. [[2026-10-12-aibs-handbook-geopolitics]]
- All three blocs back open models. [[2026-10-12-aibs-handbook-geopolitics]]
