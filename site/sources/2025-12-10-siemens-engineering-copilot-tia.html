TL;DR

  • Short trade-press news item: at the SPS 2025 fair, siemens demonstrated Engineering Copilot TIA, a generative AI assistant that works alongside its TIA Portal automation engineering software.
  • Engineers give natural-language instructions; the copilot generates and modifies project elements itself. Siemens contrasts this with tools that only offer suggestions.
  • Capabilities: code generation and testing, visualisation development, project localisation, workflow automation, configuration support for hardware such as drives, and in-app guidance. Each can be switched on or off.
  • It is a beta, delivered as a managed service, for TIA Portal versions 19 and 20, being tested with pilot customers.
  • A pilot customer's software head says it saves engineering time and improves quality. The article gives no figures.

Key claims

  • Siemens demonstrated copilots executing autonomous workflows with Engineering Copilot TIA, which operates alongside TIA Portal and turns natural-language instructions into generated and modified project elements. (paragraph 1)
  • The beta is delivered as a managed service and integrates with TIA Portal V19 and V20. (paragraph 2)
  • Siemens says that, unlike traditional tools that offer suggestions, the copilot carries out complex engineering tasks itself, taking over repetitive work so engineers can focus on higher-value activities. (paragraph 2)
  • Current capabilities: code generation and testing, visualisation development, project localisation and workflow automation, plus configuration support for automation hardware such as drives and contextual guidance in TIA Portal. Users can enable or disable each capability. (paragraph 3)
  • Siemens is testing the beta with pilot customers, such as the machinery manufacturer F. Zimmermann. (paragraph 4)
  • F. Zimmermann's head of software development and commissioning says the copilot saves engineering time and adds quality and innovative strength, and that customers get machines sooner. (paragraph 5, quote)

Quotes

Unlike traditional tools that offer suggestions to users, Siemens explained that the Engineering Copilot TIA autonomously executes complex engineering tasks by automatically generating and modifying project elements

Caveats

  • Vendor material in effect. The article is editorial (not labelled sponsored), but every claim comes from Siemens or its pilot customer. No independent test, no accuracy or time-saving figures, and no description of how engineers check what the copilot produced.
  • Beta. The product was a beta with pilot customers at the time of writing; capabilities may have changed since.
  • Pilot-customer testimony is one firm's view, given in a vendor announcement.
